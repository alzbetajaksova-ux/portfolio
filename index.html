Bachelor's Thesis

Healthcare Insurance Fraud Detection

using Cost-Sensitive Hybrid Models

Project Summary

This research focuses on developing an advanced analytical framework to identify fraudulent activities in healthcare insurance. Unlike traditional models that focus solely on statistical accuracy, this solution integrates Cost-Sensitive Learning to minimize actual financial losses. By combining supervised and unsupervised machine learning (Hybrid Architecture), the project aims to maximize cost savings for insurance providers and automate the detection of both known and emerging fraud patterns.

🔍 Motivation & Problem Definition

Healthcare fraud (such as phantom billing or upcoding) causes massive global financial losses, leading to higher premiums and reduced quality of care. Traditional manual audits are slow and often ineffective. I decided to tackle this issue because current academic models often ignore the economic reality—the fact that an undetected high-value fraud is far more costly for an insurer than a minor administrative error.

📚 Literature Review & Theoretical Foundation

Through a rigorous analysis of current research (including 2024 and 2025 studies), I identified three key pillars and three main challenges in modern fraud detection:

  • Technology Leaders: Models such as XGBoost and LightGBM demonstrate the highest stability and performance.
  • Handling Imbalance: The hybrid SMOTE-ENN technique, combined with Boruta feature selection, successfully addresses the extreme rarity of fraud cases in datasets.
  • Anomaly Detection: Integrating unsupervised learning (Isolation Forest) is essential for identifying "zero-day fraud" (new, previously unknown tactics).

Identified Gaps: Cost asymmetry (ignoring financial impact), low interpretability ("black-box" models), and poor integration of supervised and unsupervised approaches.

🛠️ Proposed Solution (Practical Implementation)

My work builds on these findings by proposing a Dual Detection Architecture with the following innovations:

Economic Optimization

Cost-Sensitive Learning: The model is penalized for errors proportional to their financial impact, aiming for maximum financial recovery rather than just accuracy percentages.

Hybrid Architecture

A combination of Cost-Sensitive XGBoost (for known patterns) and Isolation Forest (for new anomalies).

Transparency (XAI)

Utilizing SHAP (Explainable AI) methods to provide medical auditors with clear explanations of why a specific claim was flagged as high-risk.

Decision Module

An automated risk-level categorization system (High, Medium, Low) to streamline manual audit workflows.

📊 Dataset & Methodology

Built and validated end-to-end using the CRISP-DM methodology on the public Kaggle/CMS Medicare dataset (558,211 claims from 5,410 providers, 2008–2009). Raw claim-level data was aggregated to the provider level and engineered into 10 ratio-based risk indicators (e.g. AvgAmtPerClaim, Inpatient_Ratio, HealthRiskScore), addressing a severe 9.4% fraud class imbalance via SMOTE-ENN before training.

📈 Results & Model Performance

On the test set, the hybrid model outperformed standalone XGBoost on the metrics that matter most for fraud detection. Both models reached AUC-ROC = 0.947, but the hybrid achieves higher precision — meaning far fewer wasted audits on legitimate providers, an economically sound trade-off for insurers with limited audit capacity.

💰 Economic Impact (Key Result)

The thesis's core contribution: evaluating the model not just statistically, but economically, via Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient analysis. By auditing only the top 10% riskiest providers, the model captures 93.3% of total fraudulent financial exposure (vs. just 7.5% for random selection) — reaching a Gini coefficient of 0.759, close to the theoretical maximum of 0.784. This shows the model nearly perfectly prioritizes where audit resources will yield the highest financial recovery.
